# Is it AI?

Answer with evidence instead of a guess about writing style. Give a one-word verdict, AI, Human or Mixed, and show everything behind it.

## Look for evidence you can point at

Check the page and what the site serves alongside it. Look for these, roughly in order of weight.

- **Agent instruction files** the site serves, like files written for AI coding tools.
- **Builder badges and generator metadata** left by AI site builders.
- **Content credentials** on images that record how they were made.
- **Configuration in source maps** that names AI tools or builders.
- **Commit trailers** in a linked public repository that credit an AI tool.

Record each finding with what it is, where you found it, and how much it's worth.

## Weigh it carefully

- **Keep direct evidence apart from signatures.** A builder badge is direct. A pattern that merely suggests AI is a signature, and it counts for less.
- **Read the stack.** A site on the newest framework releases with stock photos looks like what AI builders produce by default. A site with years of accumulated marketing tools looks like neither.
- **Check the age.** A page published before AI writing became common, around the end of 2022, is evidence of a human on its own.
- **Cap the vocabulary.** Words people associate with models count for very little. People wrote that way first, and older posts by real people get flagged by detectors that judge style. Anything you can point at always outweighs writing habits.

## Say what the evidence shows, and no more

Evidence of a tool isn't evidence of authorship. An agent file shows an AI coding tool was used on the project. It doesn't show that the tool wrote the page being read. Word the verdict that way.

- **AI** when direct evidence shows AI built or wrote what's being asked about.
- **Human** when there's no meaningful evidence of AI, or the page predates it.
- **Mixed** when AI tools were clearly used somewhere, but not clearly for this.

## Write it up

1. **The verdict**, in one word, with one sentence on why.
2. **The findings.** Each one with where it was found and how much it counted, direct evidence first.
3. **What it doesn't show.** Name the limits plainly.

Verdicts can be wrong. Invite the user to push back, and if they correct you, take the correction and explain what would have changed your answer.
