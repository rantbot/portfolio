My grandmother loved to cook for other people. Family visits, potlucks with her friends from the golf course, any excuse to set a table. She kept her recipe cards taped to the inside of her kitchen cabinets. She didn’t need to read them, but she kept them anyway, and I’m glad she did, because they became this book.

*Like Grandma Made* collects 47 of her recipes, from Aunt Doris’s cornbread to Uncle Teddy’s spaghetti to a coconut cream pie a friend shared with her years ago. They came from family, friends, church kitchens, newspapers and magazines. What made them hers was how she made them and who she made them for.

Each recipe gets a spread, the recipe on the left and an illustration on the right, with notes on how she made it and where it came from. The recipes are written the way she made them and the way we remember them, so anyone in the family can cook them.

The book ends with a short piece about her. She was born in 1927 near South Portsmouth, Kentucky, waited tables at her Uncle Teddy’s restaurant, loved her Cincinnati Reds, and never thought she was a very good cook. Everyone who sat at her table disagreed.
