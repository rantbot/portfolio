I rent out my house on Airbnb sometimes, and it's full of smart devices. Wyze, Kasa, Nest, Ecobee, Ring, Govee, Eufy, Rachio, Tuya, and a handful more, plus Apple Home, Alexa, and Google Home on top. Every brand has its own mobile app. [Home Assistant](https://www.home-assistant.io/) pulls them together, and a local server, called Home Assistant Green allows it to work even when the Internet is down. But here's the thing: its interface is a terrible experience. I don't want my guests anywhere near it, and I don't think most homeowners should have to learn it either.

So I'm building [Braid](https://braidhome.com), a clean, simplified layer on top of Home Assistant that's built for Matter and enriched with AI. Home Assistant does the hard work of talking to thousands of different device brands while Braid is the part people actually see.

## A screen by the door for guests

When guests check in, they get a kiosk by the door. Mine is an old Kindle Fire running [Fully Kiosk](https://www.fully-kiosk.com/). Scan the QR code and the same screen opens the smart home on your phone, no app to download. Guests can turn on the lights, change the thermostat, find the Wi-Fi, and read the house guide and checkout steps. They can ask questions too, and the answers come only from the host's own notes.

Guests never get the locks, my workshop, or the cameras. If Braid can't answer something, the question goes to the host.

## A health check for owners

For owners, the home screen reads like a health check. What's on, what's off, what needs attention, and the outside cameras right there. From the dashboard, you can group devices by room or type, rename them, set schedules and modes, hide the ones you don't need, and move devices from one home to another. Braid even helps you organize them into the proper room. My Home Assistant had devices from another property showing up, so that last one was a big help in tidying it up.

## AI that notices things

Owners can add their own Claude or OpenAI key. Braid compares each day to what's normal for the home and flags things like sprinklers overwatering or Wi-Fi that isn't reaching a device. It suggests a fix and the owner approves it. Adding devices or changing a schedule can start with simply describing what you want.

## Why Braid

It started as Smart Stay, became Yokovo, and is now Braid. I wanted a name that meant lots of different things woven into one strong, beautiful thing. That's what a good smart home should feel like.

It works for one house or many, and for people without an Airbnb who just want a better way to run their own home. I'm testing it with a few hosts now, and early access is open.
