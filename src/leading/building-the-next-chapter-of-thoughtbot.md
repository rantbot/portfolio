The first project was small on purpose. A leading pharmaceutical company wanted help with a limited discovery engagement. They already had a long-term agency handling their important work, and we were a new name with a narrow brief.

Our team did excellent work. That performance earned us trust, and the trust gave me an opening. Over time, we expanded the relationship, took the place of the agency that had been there for years, and grew that small discovery project into $3.5 million in sold work.

That account says a lot about how I think a consultancy should grow. Earn the right to the bigger work by doing the smaller work better than anyone expects. Then make sure sales and delivery are pulling in the same direction, so that great work turns into the next opportunity.

Most of what I’ve done at thoughtbot has been about building a business where that happens on purpose.

## An established firm in a changing market

thoughtbot has built software for clients for more than twenty years, and it has earned a strong reputation. When I joined in 2023, I was one of five Managing Directors. The market around us was changing quickly. Clients were asking different questions, AI was changing what code costs to produce, and an established firm needed new ways to grow and to prove its value.

The team I inherited was capable, and it carried more overhead than the work could support. Making it healthy meant looking at everything together. How we were organized, how we won work, how we delivered it, and what we offered.

Today I lead the Americas business and serve on thoughtbot’s board. My responsibilities run from strategy, revenue and profitability to client relationships, staffing, sales, delivery and how the organization is designed. I lead through directors and discipline leaders, and I stay close to the biggest commercial opportunities and the decisions that shape the team. I’ve also led thoughtbot’s Europe, West Asia and Africa region, which gave me a wider view of how consulting works in different markets.

## Earning the bigger work

Asking a new client to commit to a large, expensive engagement before they’ve seen what you can do is a hard sell. So we made it easier to start.

We created [Shaping Sprints](https://thoughtbot.com/services/shaping-sprint) to be short, fixed-price engagements, usually one or two weeks long. They give a client a real experience of how we think, how we collaborate, and how good our product judgment is, before they make a bigger commitment. For us, they’re a chance to understand the client’s business, build credibility and find the work that will matter most.

Start focused, show enormous value, and earn a deeper partnership. It lowers the risk for clients, and it lets thoughtbot stand out through what we actually deliver instead of what we promise.

It also helped the economics. A steady path from small engagements to larger ones was a big part of turning the team I inherited into a profitable one.

I’ve closed over 90% of the Americas deals, some of them the largest in our twenty-three-year history. The pharmaceutical account is the clearest example of the model working at full scale.

## Three reorganizations

I’ve led three reorganizations at thoughtbot, each one responding to what the business needed at the time.

I worked with our CEO to reorganize into two geographic teams, one in the Americas and the other in EMEA, so that each region could operate and price for its own market. We consolidated into these two teams, strengthened each discipline, and made leadership responsibilities clearer. Engineering, design, product, and delivery each needed strong leaders who own their practice and develop their people, without losing the multidisciplinary collaboration that makes thoughtbot valuable to clients.

That meant investing in discipline leaders and giving them real ownership. It also meant making hard structural decisions about cost. Together, those changes helped make the business I inherited profitable.

## Knowing where we stand

When I arrived, thoughtbot wasn’t tracking the margin or profitability of its projects. It’s hard to make a business profitable when you can’t see which work is paying for itself, so I introduced project margin tracking.

I also introduced scorecards for each department. Every team now has a clear picture of how it’s doing, and we can talk about performance with the same numbers in front of us.

## Delivery as a discipline

I believe great consulting is measured by whether the client gets the outcome they actually need. Building what was requested is only part of that.

So I established Delivery as its own discipline. Delivery keeps every engagement focused on the client’s goals, their expectations, the business results they care about and the health of the relationship. Delivery Leads carry that accountability, while senior consultants keep leading projects and making technical and product decisions.

It connects what we sell with the value clients receive. And it has paid off commercially. A stronger focus on delivery has helped thoughtbot win new work and keep the clients we have.

## Revenue that repeats

Consulting usually lives project to project. I wanted a way to stay with clients after the initial build and to make part of our revenue more predictable.

I designed and operated a support and maintenance subscription for exactly that. In its first year, it signed 24 clients and grew to more than $1 million in annual recurring revenue.

Alongside that, I helped launch an AI and Product Innovation practice, for clients who want to explore what AI makes possible in their products.

## Choosing where to compete

After feeling downward pressure on rates, I led a transition to focus on regulated industries in 2025, including healthcare, financial services, and insurance. These are places where technical quality, reliability, product judgment and trust matter as much as speed.

That focus separates thoughtbot from lower-cost development shops. It positions us as a partner for difficult, consequential work, the kind where the cost of getting it wrong is high. The pharmaceutical account shows what that can look like when it comes together.

## Using every hour well

In any consultancy, some people are between client projects at any given time. That capacity often drifts. I wanted it pointed at the work that matters most.

I created Space Station to give consultants who aren’t on a client engagement meaningful internal work that moves the business forward. Instead of waiting for the next project, they improve how thoughtbot operates, build new capabilities and make our client work better.

I also created the [Experiments framework](/making/experiments-a-home-for-what-thoughtbot-people-build/), a structured way for people to test ideas, challenge assumptions, and share what they learn. The goal is to make experimenting a normal part of how thoughtbot works. It’s early, and it’s already starting to spark the kinds of conversations I hoped it would.

Behind both is something I care about a lot. I want consultants to build the tools they need to become the best in the world at their jobs. I try to do it too, and I’ve built a series of [tools for my own team](/writing/building-tools-for-my-team/).

AI makes this more important. When routine work takes less effort, the question I ask my team is what we can now do that we couldn’t before. Doing the same work faster is not the answer. Higher quality, bigger ambition, and more value for clients are much better ones.

## What premium should feel like

The idea that ties all of this together is still taking shape. I think premium consulting should feel like magic to the client.

It means clients feel deeply understood. The team anticipates what they need. The work is unusually thoughtful, and the results go past what they expected. Getting there takes product judgment to find the right problem, engineering that lasts, design that feels intentional, delivery that keeps everyone focused on outcomes, and tools, including AI, that let a team do more than anyone thought it could.

Some of that comes from earlier in my career. [North Kingdom](/leading/bringing-north-kingdom-to-the-us/) taught me how much storytelling and craft matter. [Wildebeest](/leading/wildebeest/) taught me to bring creative ambition to building real products. At thoughtbot, I’m trying to bring those ideas together into a consulting experience people remember.

## What changed

I joined as one of five Managing Directors, and today I’m the only one. Revenue grew 10% in my first year and 20% in my second. The team I inherited became profitable. A small discovery project became $3.5 million in sold work with a leading pharmaceutical company. A new subscription offering reached more than $1 million in annual recurring revenue and 24 clients in its first year. And a stronger delivery practice has helped us both win and keep clients.

Just as important to me are the systems that will keep paying off. How teams are structured, how we measure the work, how we win work, how we deliver it, how we use time between projects, how we test ideas and how people build the tools that make them better.

## What I believe now

Good delivery is the best sales strategy there is. Great work on a small engagement is how you earn the big one.

Change the system, and performance follows. Working harder inside a structure that doesn’t fit only gets you so far. The biggest gains I’ve seen came from changing how teams are organized, how work is won and how spare capacity is used.

Exceptional people do their best work with strong systems and better tools around them. A big part of my job is building those.

And AI should raise our ambition. The opportunity in front of consultancies is to attempt work that wasn’t possible before, and to make the experience of working with us feel a little like magic.
