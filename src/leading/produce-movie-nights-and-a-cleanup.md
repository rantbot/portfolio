## The situation

When the pandemic arrived, the things that held our neighborhood together stopped. People couldn't get to the store safely, and there was no easy way to see each other. Ladera Heights also sits at the intersection of three jurisdictions, which leaves gaps in who takes care of what.

## What I did

### Fresh produce, picked up from my driveway

I organized a neighborhood farm box pickup to help residents, including seniors, get farm-fresh food safely. They pulled up, opened the trunk, and I loaded a box of produce.

### Sixteen weeks of neighborhood movie nights

I produced a weekly drive-in movie series during the pandemic, giving neighbors a way to enjoy an evening together while keeping their distance.

It started as Friday nights in a neighbor’s driveway, with movies projected onto a white garage door and everyone bringing their own chairs, food, and drinks. That summer, as marketing chair of the Ladera Heights Civic Association, I moved it to a church parking lot at Fairfax and Slauson, with room for 30 cars and the sound sent to each car’s radio. We didn’t sell tickets. Neighbors donated what they could, and the studios, partly because of the pandemic, offered us 20 films to show. We opened with *A Beautiful Day in the Neighborhood*.

In July 2020, Wave Newspapers wrote about it. [Read the story](https://wavepublication.com/ladera-heights-offers-drive-in-movie-for-neighbors/).

### A monthly cleanup

I started a monthly cleanup by myself in 2019, to fill the gaps in maintenance. During COVID, I invited neighbors to join, and it became a way to bring people together around a shared responsibility.

In 2021, I talked about it with Steve Chiotakis on KCRW’s Greater LA. [Listen to the interview](https://www.kcrw.com/shows/greater-la/stories/tortoises-coronavirus-la-cleanup).

## What changed

The cleanup is still going seven years later. What started as a way to cover for three jurisdictions became a regular reason for neighbors to meet.
